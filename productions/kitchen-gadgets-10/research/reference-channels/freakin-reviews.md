## Who they are
- 📄 Run by James White, based in Las Vegas; reviews "As Seen on TV" products and gadgets, with a matching website
  and newsletter ([freakinreviews.com](https://freakinreviews.com/), [newsletter](https://freakinreviews.beehiiv.com/p/kitchen-gadgets-0125)).
- 📄 Positioned around honest, thorough reviews; an **on-camera host who physically tests products**.
- ⚠️ This is the biggest difference from us: Smart Finds Lab is **faceless and research-based**. We cannot borrow the
  "I tried it" authority — we must earn trust with sources, clear pros/cons and real demonstrations of what a product does.

## Video structure and storytelling
- 📄 Episodes test several gadgets and give each a verdict ("hits or misses"); the newsletter frames each product with a
  question such as *gimmick or game-changer?*
- 💭 Likely structure: premise/question → product-by-product test → verdict per product → overall wrap-up.
- 👁️ Needs viewing: segment length per product, how the verdict is shown (graphic, spoken, score).

## Opening hooks
- 💭 From titles and descriptions, hooks appear to open with **skepticism** ("does it actually work?") — a promise that
  the viewer will learn whether a product lives up to the hype.
- 👁️ Needs viewing: first 10 seconds, whether the result is teased, use of a cold open.

## What Smart Finds Lab takes (as ideas, never wording)
1. Frame every product around a **real question** the viewer has ("will this actually save time?").
2. Give **a clear verdict per product**, including who should skip it.
3. Make negatives visible — honesty is the brand.

## What we must not do
- Claim or imply hands-on testing. Use "buyers say", "reviewers found", "according to the specs".
- Reuse their title formulas word-for-word, their "hits or misses" framing, branding, or footage.
