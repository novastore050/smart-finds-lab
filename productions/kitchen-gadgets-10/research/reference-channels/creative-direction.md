## Where we fit
All three references are **on-camera, hands-on testers**. Smart Finds Lab is **faceless and research-based**. That is
not a weakness if we own it:

> **"We read the reviews, check the specs and compare the options — so you don't have to."**

We never claim to have tested anything. Our authority = **sources + honesty + clear demonstrations**.

## The Smart Finds Lab formula (🧭 our direction, informed by the references)
| Element | What we do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (0–10 s) | Name a universal kitchen frustration, then promise a payoff and tease #1 | All references lead with a viewer question or benefit (📄/💭) |
| Brand sting | 2.6 s card after the hook | Keeps the hook first |
| Each product (25–45 s) | **Problem → what it does → what buyers like → the honest downside → who it's for** | Verdict-driven, buyer-focused patterns in all three |
| Downsides | Always one real con, stated plainly | Honesty is the strongest shared trait |
| Evidence language | "buyers say", "reviewers found", "the maker says", "according to its manual" | Faceless research channel; no testing claims |
| Numbers | No prices on screen/voice; ratings only once verified on Amazon | Prices change; data honesty |
| Transitions | Short bridge lines between products ("If chopping isn't your problem, cleanup might be…") | Keeps retention through a countdown |
| CTA | One soft mid-video line + end screen; links "in the description" | Avoid pushy selling |
| Shorts | One product per Short, hook text in the first second, top 3 products | Reuses long-form footage |

## Titles (🧭)
- 45–65 characters, a number, a concrete benefit, no clickbait we can't back up.
- Patterns to avoid copying: "Do these actually work?", "Hits or misses", "We put it to the test" (they also imply testing).
- Our angle words: *actually useful*, *buyers keep*, *worth it*, *before you buy*, *researched*.

## Visual style (🧭, matches config/brand.yaml)
- Bright, clean kitchen b-roll, slow camera moves, close-ups of the problem and of the solution.
- Real product photos identify each product; AI b-roll stays generic (hands, food, the task).
- Teal accent, number badge, lower third with name + "Best for", one callout per product.
- Thumbnails: one hero product, 3–5 big words, high contrast, no fake reactions/faces.
