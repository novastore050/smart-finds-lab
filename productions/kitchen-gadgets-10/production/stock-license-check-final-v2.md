## What to check for every clip

1. **Licence:** record the clip URL, creator and the licence in force on the day you download it. Save a screenshot or PDF of the licence page. Pexels ([terms](https://www.pexels.com/terms-of-service/)) and Pixabay ([Content License summary](https://pixabay.com/service/license/), [terms](https://pixabay.com/service/terms/)) allow free commercial use, but only within their prohibited-use rules, and they can change their terms.
2. **People:** prefer hands-only or no-people clips. Both libraries say identifiable people are not covered by their licence (privacy and publicity rights) and that you are responsible for getting consent if needed. Neither guarantees a model release. Pexels forbids suggesting that people in footage endorse a product, which matters in a product-recommendation video.
3. **Brands and logos:** reject any clip with visible logos, labels, packaging or branded appliances. Pixabay bars commercial use of content with recognisable trademarks in relation to goods and services, and Pexels has a similar rule.
4. **Wrong product:** reject any clip that shows a specific gadget (for example another brand's egg cooker, chopper or thermometer) that viewers could mistake for the product we name. Product scenes are identified by the real product photo only.
5. **No implied claims:** a stock clip must not show a result we have not verified (for example a container held upside down without leaking).
6. **Keep records:** downloaded date, licence name and URL, who checked it. If a clip fails, replace it with another clip, a product-photo move or a graphic card.

Sources for the licence rules: [Pexels Terms of Service](https://www.pexels.com/terms-of-service/), [Pixabay Content License summary](https://pixabay.com/service/license/), [Pixabay Terms of Service](https://pixabay.com/service/terms/). This is a practical checklist, not legal advice.
